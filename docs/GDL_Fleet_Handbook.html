01Before you start

What the system is, who does what, and how to find anything.

GDL Fleet adds fleet management on top of standard ERPNext 16. It does not replace the standard Vehicle, Driver, Employee, Supplier or Asset records — it extends them. That matters in one practical way: a vehicle here is the same vehicle the accounts team sees in the General Ledger, so fleet operations and fleet costs never drift apart.

Finding your way

Everything lives in one workspace. From the ERPNext home screen open GDL Fleet. The top of the page is the dashboard — five figures and five charts, described in Part 14. Below it, eight cards:

Fleet roles can use the links on their own forms — fixed November 2026

A Link field is dead to a role that cannot select its target: frappe refuses the lookup before any form filter runs and shows “Insufficient Permission for …” with no way forward. This was not one field. A sweep of every editable link on every form each fleet role may fill found 21 blocked targets:

Fleet User Fleet ApproverLocation, Cost Center, Project, Department, Task, Employee, Driver, User, Fleet Vehicle Type

Each is granted select and nothing else — the narrow right that lets a field resolve and display a link. It gives no list, report, export, create or write on any of them. Hidden and read-only links were excluded from the sweep: neither opens a lookup, so neither earns a permission.

A test now asserts the rule rather than the symptoms — it covers 200 role and link combinations and fails the day a link is added to a fleet form without the matching grant.

Worth knowing: granting anything on a standard doctype makes frappe copy its shipped permissions into Custom DocPerm and read those from then on, so a future ERPNext change to the standard permissions of these doctypes will not reach this site. frappe.permissions.reset_perms(doctype) undoes that for any one of them.

One manager per location

Every site has a named MTO who approves that site's vehicle requests, and one of them — the Head Office MTO — is the final authority. The distinction matters:

WhoMay approve
The MTO for a siteRequests for that site only. Try to approve another site's request and you are refused by name.
The Head Office MTOAny site. Final authority, and the cover when a site's own MTO is away.

This is one approval, not two. Head Office having the right to approve everywhere does not mean Head Office has to approve anything. The list of sites and managers lives in GDL Fleet Settings › Location Managers (MTO).

Three habits worth forming

Submit, don't just save. A saved record is a draft and counts for nothing.
Never delete to correct. Cancel and amend, so the history survives.
Read the red box. Every refusal names the record it is protecting. Part 15 lists them all.

Fuel accounts are set per company

An account in ERPNext belongs to exactly one company, and a fuel claim is raised in the company that owns the vehicle. A single global account could therefore never serve a group running more than one company — which is what produced the Invalid Account error GDL hit earlier.

Every company that owns a vehicle gets its own row. On this system that is one:

CompanyFuel Expense Account
GreenDigital Limited55193 - Vehicle (Petroleum, Oil & Lubricants) - GDL

Add a row the day a second company acquires a vehicle. The fallback field below the table is used only for a company with no row of its own.

Two kinds of account are refused the moment you save, rather than when somebody later tries to raise a claim: a group account, which nothing can ever be posted to, and an account belonging to a different company from the one on its row.

The Chairman's Office split

GDL's chart also carries a separate Vehicle (POL) — Chairman's Office account, and Fuel Allowance accounts that sit under Salaries and Compensation rather than Office Expenses. Every vehicle currently posts to the general POL account. If the Chairman's vehicles are meant to be charged separately, that is a change worth making deliberately.

03The vehicle

The permanent record: identity, capacity, ownership, status and fuel target.

Fleet Administrator
The fleet is already loaded

All 14 vehicles are registered to GreenDigital Limited across Head Office, Jamjee, Dochula, Dagapela, Gedu, Samcholing and Jigmeling, each with its minimum KM/L from the mileage chart. Three things still need you: odometers all start at 0 and will be set by the first daily log or POL abstract; chassis numbers, engine numbers and acquisition dates are blank pending the registration documents; and no drivers are on the system yet, so no movement order or fuel abstract can name one.

Registering a new vehicle

  1. Open Vehicle Master › Vehicle and click Add Vehicle.
  2. Enter the Registration Number — this names the record and cannot be changed, so enter it exactly as on the certificate, e.g. BG-1-A2397.
  3. Fill Make, Model and Vehicle Type (Car, SUV, Bus, Truck, Utility).
  4. Set Ownership Type. For Leased or Rented, name the Lessor / Rental Supplier.
  5. Enter Opening Odometer, Engine No, Chassis No, Manufacturing Year, Capacity and Capacity UOM.
  6. Set Minimum Required Mileage (KM/L) — the fuel target this vehicle is held to. See Part 12.
  7. Set the Site, plus Department, Cost Center and Project if it belongs to one.
  8. Save. A Vehicle ID such as GDL-VEH-00003 is allotted and never changes.

Vehicle status

Vehicle Status is read-only because the system maintains it.

StatusMeaning
ActiveIn service and available for a movement order.
Under MaintenanceWith the workshop. Cannot be dispatched.
UnavailableOff the road for any other reason.
DisposedSold, written off or returned to the lessor.
Why "booked" is not a status

Whether a vehicle is booked right now is worked out from its movement orders, not stored. That way a booking and the vehicle record can never disagree. To see what is free, run Vehicle Availability.

04Drivers & assignment

A dated history of who drove which vehicle, kept rather than overwritten — and the driver's own staff record carried through to every document.

Fleet Administrator

Linking a driver to their staff record

Open the Driver record and set Employee. This single link is what lets the fleet documents show who the driver actually is. Wherever a driver is named, the system now fills in, without being asked:

DocumentFills in
Driver AssignmentEmployee, Location, Licence No.
Movement OrderDriver's Employee, Driver's Licence No.
POL AbstractDriver's Employee, Driver's Licence No.
Fleet Part IssueDriver's Employee, Driver's Licence No.
Asset Maintenance LogDriver's Employee
"Employee" means three different things

This catches people out, so it is worth stating plainly:

  • Driver Assignment.Employee is the driver's staff record.
  • Movement Order Request.Employee is the requester's — the person who wants the vehicle, who is usually not the driver.
  • Vehicle Log.Employee comes from the vehicle's responsible employee, which may be neither.

Fields that hold the driver's own record are named Driver's Employee for exactly this reason. When you are reading a report or writing a filter, check which of the three you mean.

If a fetched value looks wrong

These fields re-read the driver record every time the document is saved, so changing a driver's employee link corrects every draft document that names them. You do not have to hunt down and fix each one. Submitted documents keep the value they were approved with.

Assigning a driver to a vehicle

  1. Open Vehicle Master › Driver Assignment.
  2. Choose the Vehicle and Driver. The employee, location and licence number fill in.
  3. Set the Purpose.
  4. Pick the Assignment Type:
    • Primary — the regular driver
    • Temporary — standing in for a defined spell
    • Relief — covering a shift or single trip
  5. Enter the From Date. Leave To Date empty for open-ended.
  6. Submit. The vehicle's assigned driver updates to match.
The one rule

A vehicle may have only one Primary driver at a time. Overlapping dates are refused with "Vehicle … already has a primary driver … for an overlapping period". Temporary and Relief may overlap — that is the point of them.

Handing over: do not edit the old assignment. Set a To Date on it and create a new one starting the next day. Both records remain, and Driver Assignment History shows the chain — which is what you need when a penalty or accident has to be traced to whoever was driving.

05Daily KM

One record per vehicle per day. Everything downstream rests on this.

Fleet DriverFleet Administrator
  1. Open Operations › Daily KM / Vehicle Log.
  2. Select the Vehicle. Opening Odometer fills in from the last reading.
  3. Set the Date.
  4. Enter the Closing Odometer. Distance Travelled calculates itself.
  5. Enter Place to Travel — required. Add start location and destination if useful.
  6. Link the Movement Order if the day's running was against one; the driver is then taken from it.
  7. Set Usage and any dimensions — Site, Department, Cost Center, Project.
  8. Submit. The vehicle's current odometer moves to the closing reading.
Two guards you will meet

One record per vehicle per day. A second log for the same vehicle and date is refused — edit the existing one. A log attached to a movement order is treated separately, so two distinct movements in one day can still be recorded.

Unusually high mileage. Above 500 km you get a warning asking you to re-check the reading, then it lets you proceed.

If you cancel a log

The odometer is recalculated from whatever readings remain, rather than having the distance subtracted. Cancelling an old log cannot drag the odometer back past a newer reading.

Recording a service

  1. Open Service & Maintenance › Asset Maintenance Log.
  2. Select the Vehicle — now required — and the Location it was serviced at.
  3. Set Service Date and the Odometer Reading at service, and name the Service Provider.
  4. In Service Items, add a row per job done — Service Type, quantity, the part or material used, and its cost.
  5. Enter Labour Cost. Parts Cost and Total Cost add themselves up.
  6. Set Cost Center or Project if the expense belongs to one.
  7. Check Next Service Date and Next Service KM, worked out from the maintenance rules in Part 2.
  8. Write what was done in Service Description.
  9. If an outside workshop did the work, fill the Service Bill section — see Part 7.
  10. Submit.
Why the Vehicle is now required

A service record with no vehicle joins no service history and appears on no due list. It was possible to save one. It is not any more.

For a repair, use Asset Repair with the Repair / Failure Date, Maintenance Category (Corrective, Preventive, Accident) and the parts consumed. The repair cost is derived by ERPNext from those parts and the invoices, so it is not typed in.

To stay ahead, run Service Due — it lists everything approaching by date or kilometres and highlights overdue items. Notifications also go out 14 days before and the day after a service is missed.

07The service bill

A vehicle goes to an outside workshop and comes back with a bill. The bill number, its date and a scan of it live on the same service record as the work.

Fleet Maintenance UserFleet Administrator
Simplified — November 2026

This part used to describe a four-step chain: submit the bill, have it approved by a Bill Approver, raise a Purchase Order and Invoice from the approval, then watch the payment status. The final document asks for an Invoice/Reference No on the service record — not a procurement chain — so the chain is switched off.

It could not run anyway. Raising a purchase document needs a Purchasing Company and a Maintenance Service Item, and neither has ever been set, so every completed service stopped at Purchasing: Pending with an error nobody could clear. One record on staging was sitting there; it has been cleared.

Nothing was deleted. The fields, the approval code and the purchasing code are all still in place. Switching Raise Purchasing Document on Bill Approval back on in GDL Fleet Settings, with those two settings filled in, brings the whole chain back.

Recording the bill

  1. Open the maintenance log for that visit.
  2. Set the Service Provider — the workshop, as a Supplier.
  3. In Service Bill, name the Driver who took the vehicle in. Their employee record fills itself.
  4. Enter the Invoice / Reference No and Bill Date exactly as printed.
  5. Attach a scan or photograph in Bill Attachment.
  6. Enter Labour Cost and the per-item costs in Service Items. Total Cost adds itself up.
  7. Submit.
The invoice number is asked for once

It used to appear both on the record and on every service line. There is one Invoice / Reference No now, on the record.

Who may enter costs

A Fleet Driver can record the service — vehicle, odometer, date and the work done — but the cost and bill fields sit above a driver's permission level and are greyed out for them. The person who took the vehicle in is not the person who records what it cost. That separation is deliberate and is unchanged.

Paying it

Accounts

Accounts raise the Purchase Invoice for the workshop in the normal way, outside the fleet module, and pay it through a Payment Entry. The service record carries the bill number, the date, the scan and the cost, which is what Accounts need to match it — and what the final document asks the record to hold.

08Insurance & renewals

Every policy and certificate, history kept, five reminders — and optionally a purchase order when one expires.

Fleet Administrator
  1. Open Vehicle Master › Renewal Register.
  2. Choose the Renewal Type: Insurance, Registration, Fitness, Emission, Permit, Road Tax or Other.
  3. Select the Vehicle and Location, and enter the Policy / Certificate No.
  4. Name the Provider / Authority, and link the Supplier if the premium is paid through accounts.
  5. Enter the Premium / Fee, Effective From and Expiry Date.
  6. Attach the scanned certificate and Submit.
Renewing

Do not edit the old record. Create a new entry for the new period and set Previous Renewal to the one it replaces. The old record is marked Renewed with its particulars untouched, so the history stands up to audit.

Valid→ Due Soon→ Expired or →Renewed

Reminders go out at 14 days and 3 days before expiry, and again the day after it lapses — the schedule GDL asked for. Two further rungs exist and ship switched off; set Third Reminder or Final Reminder to any number of days to turn them on, or back to 0 to silence them again. Driving licence expiry is tracked the same way. For the whole picture, run Insurance / Renewal Due.

Raising the renewal order automatically

A daily job can raise a Purchase Order for each insurance policy reaching expiry, so the renewal is in the buying system before the cover lapses. It is switched off, and needs three things before it can be switched on:

  1. Set Insurance Renewal Item — the item code a premium is charged against.
  2. Set Default Insurance Supplier, used when the policy itself does not name one.
  3. Set Raise Renewal PO This Many Days Before Expiry. It is 0, meaning on the expiry date itself; a week or two of notice is usually more useful.
  4. Tick Raise Purchase Order When Insurance Expires.

Once on, each policy picked up gets a Purchase Order carrying the vehicle, its cost centre and project, and the record shows the order number together with a Purchasing Status and note, exactly as a service bill does. A policy that already has a live order is skipped rather than ordered twice.

An expired policy still orders

If a policy expired before anyone noticed, the renewal order is dated today rather than in the past — a required-by date in the past would be refused by ERPNext. The order still goes out; it is simply dated when it was actually raised.

09Requesting a vehicle

An employee asks; transport accepts or rejects.

Fleet UserFleet Administrator
Draft→submit Pending Review→accept Accepted or →Rejected
  1. Open Movement › Movement Order Request. It is numbered MOR-2026-00001.
  2. Your name fills in as requester. Add Employee — the person travelling, not a driver — and the Department fills in from it.
  3. Set the Site, Project, Task and Cost Center the trip belongs to.
  4. Describe the Purpose, and set Usage and Priority (Routine, Critical Business, Emergency).
  5. Enter the Passenger Count and Required Vehicle Type. You ask for a type, not a particular vehicle.
  6. If you already know who is travelling, name them in Passengers / Officials. It is optional — book first and name them later if you must — but anyone you list carries through to the movement order, so nobody retypes it.
  7. Set the travel and return date and time, the Pickup Location and Destination.
  8. Click Submit for Review.
Give at least 24 hours notice

The system measures the notice you give and records it as Lead Time Given. Inside 24 hours you must enter a Short-Notice Reason so the approver can judge it. Marking the request Emergency bypasses the rule entirely.

Requests are also given an Allocation Priority — lower is served first. Urgency decides it first; the notice you gave breaks ties, so of two equally urgent requests the one booked further ahead wins.

Reviewing a request

Fleet Approver — the MTO for that site
  1. Open the request from Movement Request Status or your notifications.
  2. Click Check Availability to list vehicles of that type free for the window, then click the row of the one you want — it fills Assigned Vehicle for you.
  3. Or pick it straight from Assigned Vehicle: the dropdown now offers only vehicles free for this window, at your own location.
  4. Set the Assigned Driver.
  5. Add Approver Remarks, then click Accept — or Reject, always with a reason, since the requester sees it.

The vehicle and driver stay editable after acceptance, so a last-minute swap does not mean starting again. Assigned By and Assigned On record who made the call and when.

You are offered your own location's fleet — November 2026

Two things used to be wrong with Assigned Vehicle. It offered every vehicle in the company, and it filtered on vehicle status alone — so a vehicle already booked for your window was offered, and you only found out when the save was refused. Check Availability knew better; the field beside it did not.

The dropdown now runs the same availability rules as that button, and shows only vehicles at a location you draw on. A user at Head Office is offered Head Office's vehicles; a user at Gedu is offered Gedu's.

WhoIs offered
Head Office MTOEvery location. The final authority approves anywhere — that is what the seat is for.
Fleet Administrator System ManagerEvery location.
A location's MTOThat location, from the Location Officers table in GDL Fleet Settings. Nothing to configure — they are already named there.
Anyone elseThe location on their User Permission. This is standard frappe, set in the desk under User Permission, and it is where a requester's site comes from.
A user with no location at allEverything. A site list is a filter on a dropdown, not a security boundary, and an empty vehicle field for everyone not yet mapped would stop the fleet working.

A group location stands for the depots beneath it, so somebody mapped to a region reaches every site inside it.

The clash rule has not moved: whatever the dropdown offers, a vehicle already booked for that window is still refused on save, before and after acceptance.

Only the site's own MTO may accept

Allocating a vehicle and accepting a request are reserved for the manager named for that site, or for the Head Office MTO. Anyone else is refused, and the message names who should act. A requester can never allocate their own vehicle — if they could, checking availability would mean nothing.

An MTO who raises a request themselves cannot accept it either. The Head Office MTO covers that case.

The chain

StageWhoAction
DraftFleet UserSubmit for Approval

Why the MTO approves twice-looking steps once

The decision was already taken when the MTO accepted the request. Submitting and approving the order is the same person confirming the dispatch they just authorised, not a second opinion — so the system lets them do both. The decision still cannot be self-approved: an MTO who raises a request of their own cannot accept it.

Rejection, and the longer chain

Reject sends the order to Rejected, where it stays editable, so the originator can correct it and click Resubmit. Nothing is retyped and nothing is lost.

A five-stage chain — Validate, Review, MTO/CEO, FAS — also exists and is switched off. Set Movement Order Approval to Full (Five Stage) in settings and run a migrate to turn it on. Orders already part-way through either chain keep their state.

Two checks before dispatch

Availability. A vehicle that is Under Maintenance, Unavailable or Disposed cannot be dispatched.

Double booking. If the vehicle is already committed for an overlapping period you are told which order clashes. Both checks run again if you edit an approved order, so widening the dates cannot slip a clash through.

Preparing an abstract

  1. Open Operations › POL Abstract. It is numbered POL-2026-00001.
  2. Select the Vehicle. The driver, their employee record and licence, the minimum KM/L, the opening odometer and the previous tank balance all fill in from the last approved abstract.
  3. Set the Abstract Date, then Period From and Period To.
    The period is whatever you declare — it is not forced to a calendar month.
  4. Leave Distance Basis as KM. Choose Hours only for hour-metered plant.
  5. Add a row per fill — memo, date, supplier, litres, rate, odometer. The Amount works itself out. Every field you need is in the table itself; no row has to be opened.
  6. Attach the supplier's bill in Supplier Bill Attachment.
  7. Enter the Total Fuel Recoupment for BOD and STCBL.
  8. Enter the Closing KM.
  9. Save, then Submit for Approval.

Filling the table quickly

The entries table shows the seven fields a fill needs, side by side:

ColumnWhat goes in it
Supply Memo No.The memo number, as printed.
DateThe date of the fill.
SupplierBOD or STCBL. Only those two are offered, and the choice decides which provider the amount is claimed from.
HRS LitersLitres supplied.
Rate (Nu)Rate per litre.
Odometer ReadingThe reading at the pump.
AmountLitres × Rate, calculated — and editable.
One amount, and the supplier decides where it goes

A line used to carry Amount (BOD) and Amount (STCBL) side by side. That meant saying twice what the supplier already says once — and one of the two was always empty, because a fill comes from one provider, not both.

Now there is a single Amount, and the Supplier routes it. Choose BOD and the amount is claimed from BOD; choose STCBL and it goes to STCBL. Nothing else to tick, nothing to keep in step.

The two provider columns still exist and still drive the claim, the book balance and every POL report — they are simply worked out rather than typed, and are out of sight.

A supplier that is neither provider is refused

The Supplier list offers only the two providers named in GDL Fleet Settings › BOD Supplier and STCBL Supplier. Pick anything else and the abstract will not save: "… is not one of GDL's fuel providers." A fuel line credited to neither provider cannot be claimed from either.

The rule only bites once both settings are filled in. Until then the system cannot know what to check against, so it stays out of the way.

Set for All Rows

A month of fills usually shares one supplier, one fuel type and often one rate. Rather than typing them into every line, click Set for All Rows above the table, fill in only the fields you want to apply, and click Apply.

Tick particular rows first and only those are changed; tick nothing and it applies to the whole table. Fields you leave blank in the dialog are left alone on every row. An amount you have already corrected by hand is never overwritten.

The supplier's bill is required

An abstract cannot leave Draft without Supplier Bill Attachment. The check runs when you click Submit for Approval — not at the approver's end — so an unsupported abstract never reaches their queue.

Abstracts approved before this rule came in are untouched. If you have a draft from before, you will be asked for the bill the first time you send it up.

How every figure is worked out

Total Fuel Supplied = sum of the litres on every row Amount = Litres × Rate (per row; overridable) → claimed from = whichever provider the row's Supplier names Total KM = Closing KM − Opening KM Total Fuel Consumed = Total Fuel Supplied + Previous Tank Balance − Present Tank Balance both tank balances are hidden and sit at 0, so this equals Supplied Performance per Litre = Total KM ÷ Total Fuel Consumed Closing Book Balance = Opening Book Balance + Recoupment − Amount (BOD and STCBL kept separate)
The consumption formula, and why this order

Consumed is supplied + previous − present. Fuel you had at the start plus fuel you bought, less what is still in the tank, is what you actually burned. Reversing the two balances gives a different and wrong answer.

The tank balances are off the form

Previous Tank Balance and Present Tank Balance are hidden at GDL's request. Nothing is entered in them, so both read 0 and Total Fuel Consumed now equals Total Fuel Supplied.

That is a real change to a figure. The blueprint's own example carries 40 litres in the tank at the start and 60 at the end, giving 134.81 litres consumed from 154.81 supplied — and a performance of 8.00 KM/L rather than 6.97. With the fields hidden, a period like that can no longer be recorded as the blueprint records it.

The fields and the formula are intact, not deleted, so unhiding them restores the old behaviour exactly.

The coupon has a running balance

A coupon book is not emptied and reissued each period. Whatever is left unspent belongs to the next one, so each abstract opens where the last one closed:

Opening Book Balance = the closing balance of this vehicle's previous abstract Closing Book Balance = Opening + Recoupment − Amount

Opening Book Balance is read-only and recomputed every time you save. You cannot type over it — if you could, the running total would drift away from the abstract it came from. Four rules govern it:

  • Only an approved abstract carries forward. A draft or rejected one is not an agreed balance.
  • Each vehicle has its own coupon book. Nothing crosses between vehicles.
  • An abstract entered out of order reads only periods that ended before it began, never a later one.
  • A vehicle's first abstract opens at zero, which is what a new coupon book starts at.

Spending more than the coupon holds gives a negative closing balance. That is deliberate — it is a real debt to the provider, and hiding it at zero would lose it. To work period-by-period instead, untick Carry Forward Fuel Book Balance in settings.

Recoupment belongs to the abstract, not the row

Enter Total Fuel Recoupment once, in the Fuel and Book Balance section. That is the figure the book balance is worked out from.

The entry rows used to carry recoupment columns of their own that fed no calculation at all. They have been taken off the table so nothing can be typed into a field that discards it. Figures already recorded there are kept, and the reports have always read the abstract's value.

Amount (BOD) and Amount (STCBL) on the abstract are hidden too. They are sums of the lines, so there was never anything to type — and now that the supplier decides the split, there is nothing to read either. Both still drive the closing book balance and the invoice raised on each provider.

What is hidden, and still working
HiddenWhereStill used for

Opening 227,953 · Closing 229,032 · Previous tank 40 L · Present tank 60 L · Recoupment BOD 15,000 · STCBL 20,000. This is the vehicle's first abstract, so both opening book balances are 0.

Total KM = 229,032 − 227,953 = 1,079 km Total Fuel Consumed = 154.81 + 40 − 60 = 134.81 L Performance = 1,079 ÷ 134.81 = 8.00 KM/L Closing Bal. STCBL = 0 + 20,000 − 16,339.43 = Nu. 3,660.57 Closing Bal. BOD = 0 + 15,000 − 0 = Nu. 15,000.00

Next period those two figures arrive as the opening balances. If the following abstract recoups a further 20,000 STCBL and spends 18,000, its closing balance is 3,660.57 + 20,000 − 18,000 = Nu. 5,660.57 — the coupon's to-date amount, which is what the blueprint asks the system to maintain.

This example can no longer be entered as it stands

It depends on the tank holding 40 litres at the start and 60 at the end. Those two fields are now hidden, so both read 0 and the same period would compute 154.81 L consumed and a performance of 6.97 KM/L, not 134.81 and 8.00.

The worked example is kept because it is the blueprint's, and because it shows what the formula does when the tank balances carry a value. Unhide the two fields and the figures above return exactly.

Overriding an amount

Amount is calculated but stays editable. If the approved memo shows a different figure from the raw multiplication, type the memo's figure in — the row is flagged Amount Overridden and your figure carries through. The system will not quietly re-round an approved amount, and Set for All Rows will not overwrite it either.

This matters more than it looks. The blueprint's own sheet reads 4,413.04 where 41.81 × 105.55 comes to 4,413.0455. The approved figure is the one that counts, and it survives untouched.

The rule is the same whichever provider the line is claimed from — BOD and STCBL no longer behave differently, because there is only one amount now.

Claiming the fuel from the provider

Fleet HR ApproverAccounts

Once the abstract is approved, open it and use the Fuel Claim menu:

  1. Click Raise BOD Claim or Raise STCBL Claim.
  2. A draft Purchase Invoice is created for that provider's amount only, carrying a link back to the abstract.
  3. Accounts check it and submit it. That submission is what reaches the ledger.
Fuel reaches the accounts once

The abstract itself posts nothing — it is the approval document. The invoice posts. One claim per provider per abstract; if a claim was raised in error, Accounts can cancel it and it can be raised again.

Printing the abstract

Open the abstract and click the printer icon in the right-hand sidebar — or Ctrl+P, or ⋯ › Print. The POL Abstract format is the default, so it comes up without choosing anything. Get PDF downloads it.

It is laid out to match GDL's own printed form, so a printout can be set beside the paper version and read as the same document: the ruled outer box, the company logo top right, numbering as 1.00, 2.00, dates as 05.11.26, the grey ruled value boxes down the summary, and the Submitted By / Approved by block ending in Driver and Head-HR.

The printed supply table
Sl No · Supply Memo No. · Dateas entered
HRS Liters · Rate (Nu)as entered
Amount (Nu)one money column, then a Total row
Two differences from the paper form, both deliberate

One Amount column, not two. The paper sheet has Amount (BOD) and Amount (STCBL) side by side and one of them is always empty. The printout carries a single Amount and a single Total, which is the sum of both providers — no money is lost in the change.

The units are corrected. The paper form labels the odometer km/hr and performance ltrs. A distance is not a speed and kilometres per litre is not a volume, so the printout reads km and KM/L. Everything genuinely measured in litres — the tank balances, total fuel consumed — still reads ltrs.

What the printed lines no longer say

The supply table does not name the supplier, so on an abstract mixing BOD and STCBL fills the page shows the split totals in the summary without showing which lines produced them. The provider is still on screen, in the POL Supplier / Book Balance report, and on the invoice raised against each provider.

12Mileage monitoring

Every vehicle has a fuel target. Straying from it in either direction needs HR's agreement.

Fleet HR ApproverFleet Manager

Each vehicle carries a Minimum Required Mileage (KM/L). When a POL abstract is saved, actual performance is compared against it:

Deviation % = (Actual KM/L − Minimum KM/L) ÷ Minimum KM/L × 100
The tolerance works both ways

A deviation of more than ±10% needs a reason. Running below target is the obvious case. Running far above it is flagged too, because in practice that almost always means a missed fill or a mis-keyed odometer rather than a genuinely frugal vehicle.

For a vehicle with a target of 8.00 KM/L the acceptable band is 7.20 to 8.80. Outside it, the abstract will not save until you enter a Deviation Reason, which HR reviews as part of approving it.

StatusMeaning
Within ToleranceInside ±10% of target. Nothing to do.
Below ToleranceUnder target by more than 10%. Reason required.
Above ToleranceOver target by more than 10%. Reason required — check the readings.
Not ApplicableNo target set. Excluded from monitoring.
Electric vehicles

The BYD EV at Head Office has a minimum of 0, because kilometres per litre means nothing for a vehicle that burns no fuel. A target of zero is read as efficiency does not apply, so EVs are excluded from deviation monitoring and never held up.

To see the whole fleet at once, run Mileage Deviation Dashboard. It shows actual against target for every vehicle with a chart, flags which need HR approval, and has an Outside Tolerance Only filter.

Issuing a part or tyre

  1. Open Parts & Tyres › Fleet Part Issue. It is numbered FPI-2026-00001.
  2. Select the Vehicle, Location and Issue Date.
  3. Name the Driver; their employee record and licence number fill in.
  4. Enter the Odometer at Issue — this becomes the starting reading for any tyre fitted.
  5. Set the Purpose: Routine Replacement, Breakdown, Accident, Wear and Tear or Other.
  6. Add a row per item with quantity, serial number where it has one, and rate.
  7. For a tyre, tick Is Tyre and set the Position.
  8. Set the Source Warehouse, Cost Center and Project.
  9. Submit.
The stock stays in ERPNext

Submitting raises a standard Material Issue Stock Entry from the source warehouse. The fleet module keeps no quantities of its own, so it can never drift away from the stock ledger. Where a part was bought out of pocket, link the Expense Claim instead.

One tyre per row

Tyres must be issued one at a time. Four tyres means four rows, each with its own position and serial number — that is what lets the system track the kilometres each one achieves. A tyre already recorded as fitted cannot be fitted again.

The life of a tyre

Submitting an issue with a tyre on it opens a Vehicle Tyre record (TYR-2026-00001) at status Fitted, stamped with the odometer.

When the tyre comes off, open that record and enter the Removed Date, Removed Odometer and Removal Reason. The system computes:

KM Achieved = Removed Odometer − Fitted Odometer
Target20,000 km
Tolerance± 20 %
Acceptable band16,000 – 24,000 km
Below toleranceunder 16,000 km
Above toleranceover 24,000 km

A tyre that falls short is a procurement or road-condition question. One that runs far beyond the band usually means the removal reading was missed, so both ends are reported. Run Tyre Mileage Report to see them all.

A note on where things live

This module adds to standard ERPNext rather than copying it. Vehicles, drivers, employees, suppliers, locations, cost centres and projects are the same records the rest of the business uses, and Vehicle is registered as an accounting dimension — which is why a vehicle can be selected on an invoice, an expense claim or a journal entry, and why Cost by Vehicle reports real money without a separate fleet ledger. Standard reports such as the General Ledger and Budget Variance can be filtered by vehicle for the same reason.

GDL Fleet Extension for ERPNext 16. Thresholds, reminder days, maintenance intervals, mileage and tyre tolerances, booking rules and both purchasing automations are all configuration — change them in GDL Fleet Settings rather than asking for a code change.
